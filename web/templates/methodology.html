How we measure

Text and words

We take each law's latest compilation from the Federal Register of Legislation in EPUB form, and split it into sections using the paragraph styles the Office of Parliamentary Counsel applies. A word is any run of characters between spaces, in the section's heading and text. Notes, tables of contents and endnotes are not counted.

The Constitution's figures cover the Constitution itself (sections 1 to 128 and the Schedule). The nine covering clauses of the Act that contains it are counted separately.

Compilations and amendments

The Register lists every version of a law: each period in which its text stayed the same, with the laws that changed it at the start of that period. For most versions it publishes a compilation, the text with every amendment to that date incorporated. Some periods, mostly before 2004, have no compilation. We count compilations and amending laws as the Register reports them.

Acts

Every Act on the Federal Register of Legislation, in force or not, with its compilations and the laws the Register lists as amending each one. A principal Act sets out the law on a subject. An amending Act changes other Acts; the Register classes each Act as one or the other. An amending Act stays in force until a later Act repeals it, even after its changes have been made.

Pages are the page count of the PDF the Register publishes for each compilation, summed across volumes for Acts printed in more than one. An Act's pages today are those of its latest compilation with a PDF. For the pages in force on a past date, a version with no PDF takes the count of the latest earlier compilation that has one. Page counts for older compilations aren't always published; pages-in-force figures cover only Acts with a count, and each chart says how many that is.

Amended by counts the distinct laws the Register names as having changed an Act across all its compilations. Last changed is the start of its latest version already in force; changes passed but not yet in force are listed on the Act's page.

Portfolios

The Register lists the department or departments that administer each Act in force, following the current Administrative Arrangements Order. Each department belongs to a portfolio. When the Order moves an Act to another department, the Register's listing changes with it, so the figures describe today's arrangements, not those when the Act was made.

An Act administered by more than one portfolio counts once in each portfolio's number of Acts, and its pages are split equally between them. The pages for all portfolios therefore add up to the pages in force.

Offences and penalties

We read the latest compilation of every principal Act in force and match the fixed phrases Commonwealth drafting uses. Every count is of words a reader can find in the Act; none rests on our judgement.

  • An offence is a penalty the Act sets for breaching one of its provisions. Most Acts state it at the foot of the section or subsection ("Penalty: Imprisonment for 2 years."); section 4D of the Crimes Act 1914 makes a breach of such a provision an offence. Older Acts state the punishment in the sentence instead ("commits an offence punishable, on conviction, by a penalty not exceeding 60 penalty units"), and those count too. A general rule about offences against the whole Act ("A person who commits an offence against this Act is punishable … by a penalty not exceeding the penalty applicable to the offence") doesn't.
  • A civil penalty is a provision ending "Civil penalty: …", for which a court can order a person to pay a sum without convicting them.
  • Tables of penalties. A few Acts list their penalties in a table instead: Schedule 3 of the Corporations Act 2001 sets the penalty for each offence, and its section 1317E lists the civil penalty provisions; section 539 of the Fair Work Act 2009 lists its civil remedy provisions with a maximum penalty for each. Each provision a table names counts once, as it would with a penalty at its foot, and a provision that is both footed and listed counts once. The amounts for the Corporations Act's civil penalties come from a formula in section 1317G, so they count with no size.
  • Severity is the longest term of imprisonment and the most penalty units in the penalty's words. Where a penalty differs for individuals and bodies corporate, we take the longest and the largest. Penalties stated in dollars or by a formula have no size.
  • Strict and absolute liability are counted where the Act declares them ("An offence against subsection (1) is an offence of strict liability"), once for each section. The notes that point to the Criminal Code aren't counted.
  • Burdens on a defendant are each place the Act, or its notes, says a defendant bears an evidential or legal burden.
  • Powers to make instruments are each time an Act says something may be done "by legislative instrument"; powers to make regulations are each provision that says the Governor-General "may make regulations". A Henry VIII clause lets regulations, rules or another instrument modify or amend the Act itself.

Known gaps: offences in an Act's text that refer to a penalty set elsewhere, such as breaches that section 1311 of the Corporations Act makes offences with no penalty in Schedule 3, aren't counted. The Register publishes a few dozen principal Acts in force, mostly Loan Acts, as scans of the printed Act; we can't read them, and the offences page says how many. Every penalty we count is in the CSV, with the provision it belongs to and its words, so each can be checked against the Act.

Governments

An Act is matched to the government in office on the day it was made (the day of Royal Assent), using each prime minister's term and party. A government's term starts on the day its ministry was sworn in; the dates match the Parliamentary Handbook's list of ministries. We group governments as Labor or non-Labor. Non-Labor covers the Protectionist, Free Trade, Commonwealth Liberal, National Labor, Nationalist, United Australia, Country and Liberal parties. Billy Hughes led a Labor government until November 1916, then National Labor and Nationalist governments.

Most Acts pass with votes from more than one party, so the government of the day isn't the only side that made them. We plan to add who introduced each Bill and how each party voted.

Referendums

Dates, subjects, national results and the States in favour are from the AEC's fact sheet on past referendums. State percentages are from the Parliamentary Handbook. A proposal is carried when a majority of all voters and a majority of voters in at least four States vote yes. The results for the 2023 referendum are from the AEC's official count.

Questions that need judgement

As we add Acts, some questions can't be answered by counting alone, such as whether an offence protects an identifiable person. For these, we will publish each question, the answer for every law, the words of the law the answer rests on, and how the answer was reached, including where an AI model drafted it and how often our own review agreed with it.
